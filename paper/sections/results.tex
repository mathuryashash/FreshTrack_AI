\subsection{Leakage from augmented copies}
Fig.~\ref{fig:leak} and Table~\ref{tab:leak} compare the protocols. With a
per-file split, every EfficientNet-B0 seed classifies every test image
correctly (\NaiveFreshAcc\% on freshness, \NaiveTypeAcc\% on produce type).
Keeping clusters together brings this down only slightly, to \MtlFreshAcc\% and
\MtlTypeAcc\%. The colour-histogram baseline tells a different story. Its
freshness accuracy drops from \HistNaiveFreshAcc\% to \HistGroupedFreshAcc\%,
and its produce-type accuracy from \HistNaiveTypeAcc\% to
\HistGroupedTypeAcc\%. On a per-file split, a model that sees nothing but colour
looks almost as good as a CNN, which hides how much of the task the CNN is
actually solving.

\subsection{Capture sessions}
Grouping the copies of each photograph is not enough to make the test set
independent of training. Going by the file names, the whole dataset was
photographed in roughly \NSessions{} capture sessions (a date or a camera series
within a class), and \SessionSharePct\% of the grouped-split test images come
from a session that also supplies training images. \NSingleSessionStrata{} of
the 12 produce/freshness strata come from a single session. For the
\NHeldOutStrata{} strata with more than one session we built a second split in
which one whole session per stratum is held out for testing
(\NSessTrain{}/\NSessVal{}/\NSessTest{} images). We remove the
\NSessExcluded{} training images that are augmented copies or near-duplicates
(within 2 bits) of a test image, so no training image is that close to any test
image. Clusters can still cross this split, because chained merging links
different photographs from the same session. That dependence is exactly what
the protocol is meant to probe.

On unseen sessions, freshness accuracy falls to \SessFreshAcc\% (from
\MtlFreshAcc\%), while produce-type accuracy holds at \SessTypeAcc\%.
Calibration suffers more than accuracy: freshness ECE rises from
\MtlFreshEce\% to \SessFreshEce\%, so the model is often confidently wrong.
Fig.~\ref{fig:sessions} shows where the errors come from. Averaged over the
\NSessCommonStrata{} strata that also appear in the grouped test, accuracy falls
from \SessStrataGroupedFreshAcc\% to \SessStrataHeldFreshAcc\%, but the drop is
far from uniform. Apple, bitter gourd, orange and stale-tomato sessions transfer
almost perfectly. The failures are capsicum (\SessHeldCapsicumFresh\% on an unseen fresh session,
\SessHeldCapsicumStale\% on an unseen stale one) and fresh tomato
(\SessHeldTomatoFresh\%).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/sessions.pdf}
\caption{Freshness accuracy of EfficientNet-B0 (three seeds pooled) on each
held-out capture session; $n$ is the number of held-out images. Red bars fall

\label{fig:sessions}

These are precisely the cases in which the held-out session's capture source
appears in training only with the opposite label. Every stale capsicum left in
training is a WhatsApp-compressed photograph, and every camera (IMG-series)
capsicum in training is fresh. Camera photographs of stale capsicums are duly
called fresh. In the same way, all IMG-series tomatoes in training are stale,
and the held-out IMG-series fresh tomatoes are often called stale. We read this
as a sign that the network has partly learned the acquisition source rather
than freshness. Neither a per-file nor a per-photo split can reveal it, since
\SessionSharePct\% of their test images share a session with training images.

\subsection{Multi-task versus single-task, and the choice of backbone}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the grouped-split results. The multi-task
EfficientNet-B0 reaches \MtlFreshAcc\% on freshness and \MtlTypeAcc\% on
produce type; the single-task networks reach \FreshFreshAcc\% and
\TypeTypeAcc\%. The gaps are under one percentage point, they point in opposite
directions, and three seeds cannot separate them. We see no evidence that joint
training helps or hurts, but one shared network does halve the inference cost.
MobileNetV3-Large matches EfficientNet-B0 (\MnvFreshAcc\% and \MnvTypeAcc\%)
with \MnvParams{}\,M parameters and runs in \MnvCpuMs{}\,ms instead of
\MtlCpuMs{}\,ms, so it is the model we deploy. For a single seed-0 run, the
95\% group-bootstrap interval of freshness accuracy is \MtlSzeroFreshCI{}\%. It
is that wide because only the \NTestGroups{} test clusters, not the \NTest{}
images, are independent.

\subsection{Cross-dataset produce type}
On \NCross{} external images of five of the six produce types (bell pepper
counts as capsicum, and there is no bitter gourd), produce-type accuracy is
only \MnvCrossTypeAcc\% for MobileNetV3 and \MtlCrossTypeAcc\% for
EfficientNet-B0, against roughly 99\% in-domain. The external photos carry no
freshness labels. MobileNetV3 calls \MnvCrossShareFreshExact\% of them fresh
and EfficientNet-B0 \MtlCrossShareFreshExact\%, which is plausible for retail
photos but is not an accuracy.

\subsection{Out-of-distribution gate}
Table~\ref{tab:ood} compares the post-hoc scores, and Fig.~\ref{fig:ood} shows
the energy scores of the deployed model. Scores computed on the six-way
produce-type head separate supported from unsupported images much better than
scores on the two-way freshness head: near-OOD AUROC is \MnvEnergyNearAuroc\%
for energy on the type head and \MnvMspFreshNearAuroc\% for MSP on the
freshness head. With only two classes, entropy is a monotone function of MSP
and ranks inputs identically. The earlier version of the system rejected an
image when freshness entropy exceeded 1.5 bits or the top probability fell
below 0.3. Neither can happen with two classes (entropy is at most 1 bit and
the top probability at least 0.5), so that gate never fired.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/ood.pdf}
\caption{Energy scores of the deployed model. The dashed line is the gate
threshold, set to accept 95\% of validation images. Unseen produce overlaps
the accepted region far more than CIFAR-10 images do.}
\label{fig:ood}
\end{figure}

At a threshold that keeps 95\% of validation images, the MobileNetV3 energy
gate, averaged over three seeds, accepts \MnvGateTestIdAcceptRate\% of
in-distribution test images and rejects \MnvGateFarOODRejectRate\% of CIFAR-10
images, but only \MnvGateNearOODRejectRate\% of unseen produce
(\MnvGateNearMin--\MnvGateNearMax\% across seeds; for the deployed seed,
\DeployGateAccept\%, \DeployGateFar\% and \DeployGateNear\%).
Fig.~\ref{fig:ood} makes the problem visible. Most CIFAR images fall well below
the threshold, while unseen fruit and vegetables straddle it. A photo of a
vegetable the model has never seen is the hard case.
