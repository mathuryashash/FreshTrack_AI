\section{Introduction}
In informal retail settings, fruit and vegetables are usually sorted by eye,
without instrumented grading. A phone photograph that flags stale produce is an attractive
low-cost aid, and public image datasets of ``fresh'' and ``rotten'' or
``stale'' produce have made the task a popular benchmark: recent papers report
accuracies close to 100\%~\cite{noisyvit2025}.

Those numbers deserve scrutiny. Several of the most-used Kaggle releases ship
\emph{pre-augmented} images: each source photograph appears many times as a
rotated, flipped, translated or noise-corrupted copy. A split drawn per
\emph{file} then places near-copies of a test image in the training set, the
classic leakage failure mode described by Kaufman \emph{et al.}~\cite{kaufman2012leakage}
and surveyed across applied ML by Kapoor and Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}.

This paper studies freshness and produce-type recognition under a protocol that
removes this leakage, and reports what remains. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{leakage audit} of the Kaggle ``Fresh and Stale Images of Fruits
and Vegetables'' dataset~\cite{kaggle_fresh_stale}: its \NImages{} images collapse into
\NGroups{} near-duplicate clusters (\NFilenameGroups{} by filename alone), and a
per-file split puts a sibling of \NaiveLeakPct\% of test images into training.
A second, held-out-session analysis shows that the remaining in-domain accuracy
partly rests on capture-session cues. We release both splits.
\item A controlled comparison of a \textbf{multi-task} network (freshness +
produce type) against single-task networks with identical backbones, and of
EfficientNet-B0~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} against MobileNetV3-Large~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3},
over three seeds, with a group-bootstrap confidence interval for a single run.
\item An evaluation of \textbf{post-hoc out-of-distribution (OOD) gates} for
rejecting non-produce and unseen-produce photos, against an external dataset
and CIFAR-10.
\item An honest deployment design: attributes that have no ground truth in any
available dataset (quality grade, remaining shelf life) are \emph{not} learned;
they are reported as explicitly labelled heuristics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Freshness classification.} CNN and transformer classifiers trained on
public fresh/rotten datasets report accuracies in the high 90s. Transfer-learned
AlexNet reaches 99.8\% on a public fresh/rotten dataset~\cite{amin2023freshness},
and a multi-task ResNet ensemble predicts freshness and fruit type jointly
(98.5\% and 97.4\%)~\cite{kang2022ensemble}. Closest to our setting,
Multi-Task NoisyViT~\cite{noisyvit2025} reports 99.85\% on the same
Fresh/Stale dataset we use, with random 60/20/20 splits; the only
deduplication it describes is MD5 hashing, applied when building its merged
Freshness44 set. In~\cite{amin2023freshness} the data are
rotation-augmented \emph{before} a random 80/10/10 split. None of these works
groups augmented or near-duplicate copies of a source photo before splitting,
and we found no freshness study that measures the resulting leakage.

\textbf{Shelf-life estimation.} Predicting remaining shelf life requires
measured end points. Datasets that follow produce over time until spoilage
exist~\cite{fruqdb2022}, and the recent CNN+DeiT study of~\cite{shelflife2026arxiv}
forecasts spoilage with a symmetric mean absolute percentage error above 40\%.
The dataset we use has no such measurements, so we treat shelf life as out of
scope for learning.

\textbf{Multi-task learning.} Sharing a representation across related tasks
can act as an inductive bias~\cite{caruana1997multitask,ruder2017overview};
whether it helps depends on task relatedness, which we test directly.

\textbf{OOD detection.} Maximum softmax probability (MSP)~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}
and the energy score~\cite{liu2020energy} are standard post-hoc detectors that
need no retraining, which suits on-device use.

\textbf{Leakage.} Kaufman \emph{et al.}~\cite{kaufman2012leakage} formalise
leakage; Kapoor and Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} document its effect on
published results across fields, including duplicated samples across splits.

\section{Data and Leakage Audit}
\subsection{Datasets}
\textbf{Main set.} The Kaggle ``Fresh and Stale Images of Fruits and
Vegetables'' dataset~\cite{kaggle_fresh_stale} contains \NImages{} images of six
produce types (apple, banana, bitter gourd, capsicum, orange, tomato), each
labelled fresh or stale by folder. Both labels are used as ground truth.

\textbf{External set.} The Kaggle ``Fruit and Vegetable Image
Recognition'' dataset~\cite{kaggle_fruit_veg_recognition} (36 classes, no freshness
labels) is used only for evaluation: its apple, banana, orange, tomato,
capsicum and bell-pepper images (\NCross{}) form a cross-dataset produce-type
test, and its \NNearOOD{} images of other classes form a near-OOD set. We drop
\NExtDropped{} external images that near-duplicate a main-set image.

\textbf{Far-OOD set.} 2,000 randomly drawn CIFAR-10 test
images~\cite{krizhevsky2009cifar}, upsampled to the model input size.

\subsection{Leakage and the grouped split}
\begin{sloppypar}
File names in the main set encode the augmentation applied to a source photo
(prefixes such as \texttt{rotated\_by\_15\_}, \texttt{saltandpepper\_},
\texttt{translation\_}, \texttt{vertical\_flip\_} and \texttt{Copy of}, and
suffixes such as \texttt{.jpg\_0\_4456.jpg}). We normalise each name back to
its source and join files with the same source, within a produce type, into
one group (\NFilenameGroups{} groups). We then compute a 64-bit difference hash
of every image and merge groups whose images lie within a Hamming distance of
2 bits, again only within a produce type (low-texture images of different
produce otherwise collide). This yields \NGroups{} clusters.
\end{sloppypar}

Because merging is transitive, a single chance match can join distinct
photographs: \NMixedClusters{} clusters (\NMixedClusterImgs{} images) contain
both fresh and stale images, and the largest has \MaxClusterSize{} images.
Over-merging cannot leak test images into training; it only makes the split
coarser. We therefore call the units ``clusters'' rather than photographs.

The grouped split assigns whole clusters to train, validation and test with
stratified group $k$-fold sampling. Because some photo sessions contain
hundreds of copies, we select, among 50 candidate seeds, the split whose
per-stratum validation and test shares are closest to 15\% (seed \SplitSeed{});
the result has \NTrain{}/\NVal{}/\NTest{} images and \NTestGroups{} independent
test groups (Table~\ref{tab:split}). For comparison we also draw a per-file
split with the same proportions. Under it, \NaiveLeakPct\% of test images have
a group sibling in training.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Model}
A timm~\cite{wightman2019timm} backbone pretrained on ImageNet~\cite{deng2009imagenet}
(EfficientNet-B0 or MobileNetV3-Large) produces a pooled feature vector. Each
task has its own head (dropout 0.3, linear 256, ReLU, dropout 0.2, linear to the
task's classes): freshness (2 classes) and produce type (6 classes). The
multi-task loss is the mean of the two cross-entropy terms; single-task
baselines use the same backbone and head with one task.

\subsection{Heuristic attributes}
No available dataset contains graded quality or measured shelf life, so neither
is learned. For the user interface only, the service reports a grade from the
predicted $p=P(\text{fresh})$ (A if $p\ge0.85$, B if $p\ge0.5$, else C) and an
indicative shelf life $d_t\,p$, where $d_t$ is a room-temperature reference for
produce type $t$. Both fields are flagged as heuristic in the API and the app,
and we make no accuracy claim about them.

\subsection{OOD gate}
We compare four post-hoc scores: negative entropy of the freshness head (the
gate used by the previous version of the system), MSP of the freshness head,
MSP of the produce-type head~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}, and the energy score
(negative free energy at temperature 1) $\log\sum_k e^{z_k}$ of the
produce-type logits~\cite{liu2020energy}. The
deployed gate uses the energy score, with the threshold set to accept 95\% of
validation images; the threshold is stored with the checkpoint.

\subsection{Training and evaluation protocol}
Images are resized to $224\times224$ and normalised with ImageNet statistics.
Training augmentation: random resized crop (scale 0.8--1), horizontal and
vertical flips, 90\textdegree{} rotations, colour jitter, Gaussian noise and
coarse dropout. We train with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 64), one epoch at 10\% of the
base learning rate followed by cosine decay, mixed precision, gradient clipping at 1.0, up to 10 epochs
with early stopping on validation loss (patience 3); runs stopped after
\EpochsMin--\EpochsMax{} epochs. Each configuration is trained with seeds 0, 1
and 2 and we report mean $\pm$ standard deviation; calibration is measured with the
expected calibration error (ECE, 15 bins)~\cite{guo2017calibration}. For a single run we also
report a 95\% confidence interval from 1,000 bootstrap resamples of
\emph{test groups}~\cite{efron1994bootstrap}, since images within a group are
not independent. As a floor we train a logistic-regression classifier on
$8\times8\times8$ HSV colour histograms.

Hardware: one NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8 GB) for training; latency is
measured on the Intel Core i7-14700HX CPU with 4 threads, batch size 1, as the
median of 200 runs interleaved across all models. Software:
Python 3.13, PyTorch 2.12, timm 1.0.24, PyTorch Lightning 2.6.1,
Albumentations 2.0.8. Code, split files and per-run logs are available at
\url{https://github.com/mathuryashash/FreshTrack_AI}.

\section{Deployment}
The model is served by a FastAPI service that validates uploads (content type,
size, file signature, image decoding), applies EXIF orientation, runs the model,
applies the OOD gate, and logs predictions and optional user corrections to
SQLite for later relabelling. A Flutter client displays the freshness and
produce-type predictions, labels the grade and shelf-life fields as estimates,
and avoids food-safety wording (``looks fresh'' rather than ``safe to eat'').
The checkpoint travels with a metadata file holding the label order,
preprocessing constants and OOD threshold, so a client cannot silently use
different preprocessing. We deploy the MobileNetV3-Large multi-task seed with
the lowest validation loss. With \MnvParams{}\,M parameters and \MnvCpuMs\,ms
CPU latency, on-device inference is feasible; exporting it to ONNX Runtime for
offline use on phones is future work.
