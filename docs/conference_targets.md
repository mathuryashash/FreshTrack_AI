# FreshTrack: Where to Submit (Oct 2026 – Jun 2027)

*Compiled 25 Sep 2026 (IST) for the paper "FreshTrack: Leakage-Aware Multi-Task Learning for Produce Freshness and Type Recognition on Mobile Devices" (IEEEtran conference format).*

**How to read this document**
- **VERIFIED** means the 2026/27 deadline was read from the official CfP page, the official OpenReview entry, or an official organiser post.
- **UNVERIFIED** means the 2027 date has not been published yet. In those rows the previous year's deadline is given so you can plan, and you must confirm the date before relying on it.
- Deadlines marked AoE ("Anywhere on Earth", UTC-12) close at **17:29 IST on the following day**. Still, aim to submit a day early.
- **Fit score** is out of 5. It measures how well the topic and the level of novelty match the venue. It does not measure prestige.

Read each journal's prior-publication policy first.

## 3. Recommended submission plan

**Rule 0: one venue at a time.** IEEE, CVF and Elsevier all prohibit dual submission. Submit to the next venue only after a rejection or a withdrawal.

1. **Now → 9 Oct 2026: HARVEST-India 2026 at IEEE HiPC** (fit 5, Bengaluru, IEEE format).
   - This is the best match on topic, format and logistics. The IEEEtran draft needs only an 8-page trim.
   - It is also the fastest route to an IEEE-indexed paper: notification on 6 Nov, and the paper is presented in India in December.
   - Choose this **instead of** HARVEST-Vision (16 Oct). HARVEST-Vision needs a CVF reformat and in-person attendance in Orlando in early January, and US visitor-visa waits in India make that unrealistic.
2. **If HARVEST-India rejects (6 Nov): IEEE CONECCT 2027 (15 Dec 2026)** first, with **IATMSI 2027 (20 Dec 2026)** as the backup.
   - Use the reviewer feedback. Add error bars across the 3 seeds, a leakage-audit figure, and on-device latency numbers.
   - Confirm CONECCT's date as soon as its CfP opens. If it slips or looks doubtful, go to IATMSI; its date is confirmed.
3. **Spring 2027, stretch goal: IEEE CAI 2027 (about 15 May 2027) or ICMLA 2027 (about May 2027).**
   - This is for a strengthened version if the first two are rejected. It is also an option if the paper is accepted but you have enough new results for a clearly different follow-up.
   - A CVPR 2027 workshop (MetaFood, around March 2027) is only possible if the work is **not already published**. Accepted CVPRW papers are archival, and CVF will not accept work that has already appeared.
   - So decide in November: take the IEEE-India route now (safe), or hold the paper for a CVPR workshop (higher prestige, but US travel).
4. **Mid-2027: extend to a journal.** Send the extended version to **Smart Agricultural Technology** (US$2,110) or **IEEE Access** (US$2,160). Check whether your institution has an Elsevier or IEEE agreement that waives or discounts the APC.

## 4. Avoiding fake "IEEE" conferences

Many predatory events put "IEEE" in their name or promise "IEEE Xplore / Scopus publication" without IEEE's involvement. Some even clone a real conference's name. For example, WASET lists an "ICSPIN 2027 … Signal Processing and Integrated Networks" in Paris, which copies Amity's SPIN.

**Check every venue before paying:**
1. **Search for it on IEEE's conference search** (https://conferences.ieee.org/conferences_events/ or https://events.ieee.org/search-ieee-conferences/).
   - A legitimate event appears there with a **conference record number** (a 5-digit number, for example #6xxxx).
   - It also lists the sponsoring IEEE Section, Society or Council and whether that body is a financial sponsor or a technical co-sponsor.
   - If it is not listed, treat it as not an IEEE conference.
2. **Look up the previous edition in IEEE Xplore** under the exact same name and edition number, and check that the papers are actually there.
3. **Check the organiser.** Real Indian IEEE conferences are run by an IEEE Section (Bangalore, Delhi, MP, Kerala and so on), the India Council, or a named host institute. The host institute's domain should appear on the site.
4. **Watch for red flags:**
   - Acceptance within days.
   - A "Scopus-indexed" promise with no Xplore history.
   - Registration fees demanded before review.
   - Many unrelated topics.
   - The same organiser running dozens of conferences in different cities.
   - A domain registered recently.
   - Gmail contact addresses.
   - A deadline that keeps being "extended" every week.
5. **Know what the standard wording means.** Even genuine events say "accepted and presented papers will be *submitted* to IEEE Xplore, subject to quality review". Papers that are not presented (no-shows) are removed. IEEE can also withdraw proceedings in which it later finds paper-mill content.
6. **Use the Think.Check.Attend checklist** (https://thinkcheckattend.org/).
